Wildlife

Forty-one creatures, built from ARK skeletons

Every creature keeps its original ARK bone hierarchy and animation clips, rebuilt as Minecraft cuboids and rendered with GeckoLib. Five coloured skin variants each, herds, hunters, nests, sea creatures and cold-climate species. The pips show the first danger rank where each one appears.

%%CREATURES%%
World

A map with hidden danger

The Overworld repeats five equal-area danger zones in a symmetric grid. Curved borders make the pattern feel random on foot. The zone decides which species spawn and how strong they are. Players only see it through the Xaero map overlay, and that overlay can be locked until the tribe tames a creature from a rank-5 zone. This map runs the same formula as the mod. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom.

Move over the map

Habitats

Danger and wild levels come only from the zone. The biome decides which community lives there: warm species share every temperate biome, the snow, the wetlands and the sea keep their own residents, and flyers nest over any land. Spawn-list weights are split across each habitat, so wider habitats bring variety, not more animals.

%%HABITATS%%

Land habitats

Herds keep home ranges near water, predators patrol and hunt, and populations recover per habitat without forcing chunk loads.

Flyers and nests

Each flyer keeps one local nest on cliffs or shores. It perches, forages and returns home, and its eggs can be collected.

Sea and swamp

Sea creatures keep saved home pools. Crocodilians and Titanoboa hunt shorelines, and cold species drink from snow and ice.

Behaviour

Animals that pace themselves

Every creature runs a two-level state machine: an intent (roam, hunt, flee, sleep, drink...) and the animation-timed action that carries it out (notice, roar, chase, look around...). How much of it runs depends on the distance to the nearest player, so a valley of herds costs little until you walk into it, and herd mates react with their own delays instead of moving like one machine.

%%BEHAVIORTIERS%%

Each tier border has a margin of %%BEHAVIORMARGIN%%, so a creature on the line does not flicker between tiers. A hit, an alarm, a target, a rider, a tame or torpor always runs full detail, and hunger and thirst only change there.

The day of a land animal

Each animal shifts its own clock by up to half a minute, so a herd dozes off and wakes one by one.

%%SCHEDULE%%

State machines by model

Rows are the current state and columns the next one. A filled cell is a transition the real decision code produced in seeded encounters; hover or tap it for the rules behind it. The diagram beside it shows the same edges.

Progression

From a loose rock to the Iron Age

The Tribe Chronicle (press P in game) holds forty-four technologies over three ages. Each age starts at one gate and splits into its paths (four in the Prehistoric, three after), and every path must be finished to open the next age. Progress is shared by your FTB team. %%JOURNAL%%
Survival systems

Built for a tribe of two or more

Taming and torpor

Each creature tames one of three ways: passive feeding, knockout with narcoberries and tranquilizer arrows, or aerial feeding by appetite. Favourite foods tame faster, and every creature has a measured rider seat.

Weight and cargo

Carried mass shows on a HUD gauge. Past capacity you slow down, and overloaded flyers refuse to take off. Harnesses unlock each creature's hold, with one-click Load and Unload to nearby chests.

Working tames

Order a Triceratops to forage plants or an Ankylosaurus to mine stone and ore. They work only while a tribe member supervises, and they never touch blocks players placed.

The downed state

A lethal hit starts a bleed-out timer instead of killing you. A tribemate can revive you with a fiber bandage before it runs out.

Keratin and melee

Keratin makes the first armour set and the Keratin Spear. Better Combat gives every Ark weapon its own swing: the spear stabs two-handed with extra reach, knives slash as daggers.

Tribes

FTB Teams parties share riding, cargo, orders and work permissions on each other's tames, plus the Chronicle and the journal.

The First Guardian

Carry an Allosaur Heart to an Ancient Remnants monolith to wake a Guardian Giganotosaurus. It resets if you retreat and can't be farmed, and it drops the Workshop Schematic that opens the workshop age.

Night and behaviour

Creatures forage, drink, rest, sleep, flee and hunt on a day-night cycle. Carnivore eyes glow in the dark, and wild creatures grow stronger with level.

Spatial audio

Built-in reverb and occlusion, footsteps that match the material underfoot, and ambient soundscapes, all in one client-only engine.

Carcasses and cooking

Dinosaurs drop seven meat families plus leather, bone and feathers; horned, plated and beaked ones also drop keratin. Cook meat on the Stone Fire, or hang it on a rack and let it cure to Dried Meat III.

Camp and blocks

Every station is a real object

Original low-poly models built from Minecraft cuboids, with no custom rendering library. Every card is rendered from the model the game ships, with the same camera and light. Stations only work in loaded chunks, so being away never produces a windfall.

%%BLOCKS%%
Recipe Tree

What each item needs, from bare hands up

The recipe gates, separate from the Tribe Chronicle: which station or tool opens each item. Item sprites are crisp 32 px originals or re-toned vanilla sprites; dinosaur meats are drawn larger in hand and on the ground than normal Minecraft meat.

The workstation spine

Every station and tool gate sits on one line, left to right in the order it unlocks, from bare hands to the First Guardian. Each recipe is a column on its station: inputs above the line, outputs below. Drag sideways or jump to a station. Items made by one recipe arrive at another as labels instead of long arrows; hover or tap one to light up every recipe that uses it. This is the draft awaiting approval (F12), so it shows proposed changes beside what the game does today.

Accessories

Gear worn on the body

Every Curios slot is in use: two head slots, necklace, back, body, two bracelets, hands, two rings, belt, legs, two feet, a charm and the universal curio slot. Each accessory is a small model hung on the player's own bones, so it walks, sneaks, swims and rides with the wearer, spreads over armour, and shows on the first-person arms. Renders below come from the shipped models.

Primitive gadgets

What prehistoric people actually wore, made from creature parts and camp materials: slit goggles, Ötzi's pack frame, snowshoes, bone skates, a Beaker-culture wristguard, the Ishango tally bone. The stalker set (hood, ghillie wrap, moccasins) hides each sense the creatures use: scent, sight and hearing.

%%ACC_PRIMITIVE%%

Relics and totems

The mystical set, bound with red ochre: amber that refuses death, an alicorn against poison, the feathered-serpent mantle, the Guardian's crown, rings and creature totems.

%%ACC_RELIC%%

Creature parts

A player kill rolls the creature's part on top of its carcass (meat, hide, bone). The Tally Bone adds a Looting level against creatures.

Interface rework

Carved stone, moss and ember

The Ark UI resource pack re-skins the whole vanilla interface to match this page, so shaders and the menus share one look. Containers become light limestone, so vanilla's dark titles stay readable. Buttons, the hotbar and tooltips become dark basalt with moss and ember accents. Every slot stays exactly where vanilla puts it, so other mods' screens still line up.

Integrations

Every mod in the pack, and what Ark does with it

Each integration is one row in the management dashboard. Official releases are pinned by SHA-512 and rebranded "Ark - Name" with the Ark icon and a credit line; the original license and mod id stay intact. Where Ark needs code changes, the mod is built from its own source with Ark patches (Ark/pack/patches). python Ark/tools/build_pack.py assembles everything into a Modrinth pack.
