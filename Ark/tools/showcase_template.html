An ARK-style survival overhaul for Minecraft 26.1.2 on NeoForge: tame and ride real ARK creatures, survive a world split into hidden danger zones, and climb from a loose rock to the Iron Age with your tribe.

%%STATS%%
Wildlife

Forty-one creatures, built from ARK skeletons

Every creature keeps its original ARK bone hierarchy and animation clips, rebuilt as Minecraft cuboids and rendered with GeckoLib. Five coloured skin variants each, herds, hunters, nests, sea creatures and cold-climate species. The pips show the first danger rank where each one appears.

%%CREATURES%%
World

A map with hidden danger

The Overworld repeats five equal-area danger zones in a symmetric grid. Curved borders make the pattern feel random on foot. The zone decides which species spawn and how strong they are. Players only see it through the Xaero map overlay, and that overlay can be locked until the tribe tames a creature from a rank-5 zone. This map runs the same formula as the mod. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom.

Move over the map

Biome difficulty

Biomes carry their own difficulty on top of the zones, and wild creature levels scale with both.

%%TIERS%%

Land habitats

Herds keep home ranges near water, predators patrol and hunt, and populations recover per habitat without forcing chunk loads.

Flyers and nests

Each flyer keeps one local nest on cliffs or shores. It perches, forages and returns home, and its eggs can be collected.

Sea and swamp

Sea creatures keep saved home pools. Crocodilians and Titanoboa hunt shorelines, and cold species drink from snow and ice.

Progression

From a loose rock to the Iron Age

The Tribe Chronicle (press P in game) holds forty technologies over three ages. Each age starts at one gate and splits into three paths, and all three must be finished to open the next age. Progress is shared by your FTB team. Hover a node for its objective.

%%JOURNAL%%
Survival systems

Built for a tribe of two or more

Taming and torpor

Each creature tames one of three ways: passive feeding, knockout with narcoberries and tranquilizer arrows, or aerial feeding by appetite. Favourite foods tame faster, and every creature has a measured rider seat.

Weight and cargo

Carried mass shows on a HUD gauge. Past capacity you slow down, and overloaded flyers refuse to take off. Harnesses unlock each creature's hold, with one-click Load and Unload to nearby chests.

Working tames

Order a Triceratops to forage plants or an Ankylosaurus to mine stone and ore. They work only while a tribe member supervises, and they never touch blocks players placed.

Recovery and the downed state

A lethal hit starts a bleed-out timer instead of killing you. A tribemate can revive you with a fiber bandage. Real deaths leave a recovery cache that keeps your loot safe until you collect it.

Tribes

FTB Teams parties share riding, cargo, orders and work permissions on each other's tames, plus the Chronicle and the journal.

The First Guardian

Carry an Allosaur Heart to an Ancient Remnants monolith to wake a Guardian Giganotosaurus. It resets if you retreat and can't be farmed, and it drops the Workshop Schematic that opens the workshop age.

Night and behaviour

Creatures forage, drink, rest, sleep, flee and hunt on a day-night cycle. Carnivore eyes glow in the dark, and wild creatures grow stronger with level.

Spatial audio

Built-in reverb and occlusion, footsteps that match the material underfoot, and ambient soundscapes, all in one client-only engine.

Carcasses and cooking

Dinosaurs drop seven meat families plus leather, bone and feathers. Cook meat on the Stone Fire, or hang it on a rack and let it cure to Dried Meat III.

Camp and blocks

Every station is a real object

Original low-poly models built from Minecraft cuboids, with no custom rendering library. Stations only work in loaded chunks, so being away never produces a windfall.

%%RENDERS%%
  • Loose Rock: lies on the ground in every Overworld biome and is picked up by hand. The first resource of the game.
  • Stone Fire: holds fuel and is lit with a fire starter. Cooks four items on a spit, turns sticks into torches and heats a pot on top.
  • Primitive Forge: the furnace replacement. Runs every non-food smelting recipe, other mods' included.
  • Drying Rack: stacks upward. Turns meat into Dried Meat I, then cures it to II and III.
  • Cooking Pot: two prepared meals. Needs a lit fire below.
  • Feeding Trough: ten wood finishes. Feeds hungry tames nearby.
  • Field Bedroll: sets your respawn point and keeps it even if the bedroll is destroyed.
  • Recovery Cache: marks where you died. The loot lives in world data, so breaking the marker never loses it.
  • Nests and berry bushes: five flyer nests and four plantable Ark berries.
Items

The inventory, in pixels

Item sprites are crisp 32 px originals or re-toned vanilla sprites. Dinosaur meats are drawn larger in hand and on the ground than normal Minecraft meat.

%%ITEMS%%
Interface rework

Carved stone, moss and ember

The Ark UI resource pack re-skins the whole vanilla interface to match this page, so shaders and the menus share one look. Containers become light limestone, so vanilla's dark titles stay readable. Buttons, the hotbar and tooltips become dark basalt with moss and ember accents. Every slot stays exactly where vanilla puts it, so other mods' screens still line up.
