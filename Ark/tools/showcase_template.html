Wildlife

Every creature keeps its original ARK bone hierarchy and animation clips, rebuilt as Minecraft cuboids and rendered with GeckoLib. Five coloured skin variants each, herds, hunters, nests, sea creatures and cold-climate species. The pips show the first danger rank where each one appears. World

Hidden danger over %%BIOMES_COUNT%% biomes

The Overworld repeats three danger zones in a symmetric grid: the easy zone around the spawn holds a fifth of the land, the dangerous and the deadly zone two fifths each, and curved borders make the pattern feel random on foot. The zone decides which species may appear and how strong they are; the biome decides who lives there. Players only see the zones through the Xaero map overlay, and that overlay can be locked until the tribe tames a creature from the deadly zone.

The map runs the same formula as the mod: drag to pan, scroll to zoom. Beside it is every biome the pack generates, Minecraft's own and the ones Terralith adds, with what spawns there, counted by community; open a card for the names. The dot beside a name is the danger rank where that creature first appears.%%BIOMES_EMPTY%% The Nether and the End are closed in this pack.

Behaviour

One animal, two state machines

A wild animal is a body while a player is near and a record on the wildlife register the rest of the time. The body runs the behaviour machine: what it does from one half second to the next, as much of it as the distance to the player pays for. The record runs the existence machine: whether it fed, bred, was hunted or died while its chunk was not loaded. One hands the animal to the other as the chunk loads and unloads, hunger included.

Behaviour state machine

The animal as you meet it. A two-level machine: an intent (roam, hunt, flee, sleep, drink...) and the animation-timed action that carries it out (notice, roar, chase, look around...). Herd mates react with their own delays instead of moving like one machine.

Detail by distance

The nearer the player, the more of the machine runs, so a valley of herds costs little until you walk into it.

%%BEHAVIORTIERS%%

Each tier border has a margin of %%BEHAVIORMARGIN%%, so a creature on the line does not flicker between tiers. A hit, an alarm, a target, a rider, a tame or torpor always runs full detail, and hunger and thirst only change there.

The day of a land animal

Each animal shifts its own clock by up to half a minute, so a herd dozes off and wakes one by one.

%%ACTIONS%%

Existence state machine

The animal while nobody is near. It has no body and takes no server time: the register of its biome region is lived on by rounds, once a game day. Here those rounds run without the game, years in a second, to see whether a region keeps within its bounds, keeps every kind of animal and settles, and to tune the weights until it does.

A region and its years

A biome region is a connected patch of one biome inside a tile of 32 by 32 chunks. Who lives in each sample biome is read from the spawn tags the game uses; the run is repeated from different draws, and the verdict speaks for all of them.

The life of a record

Between two rounds a record is fed, hungry or starving: the hunger its body will have when the chunk loads. It comes to the region with its group or is born there, and it ends hunted, of age or starved. Each connection carries its weight: how often it was taken in the runs above, and under it what sets it.

    The bounded functions

    The proposal. Each round the region counts its register and reduces it to one number for each kind of eater, its pressure. Every daily odd is a straight line through that number, clamped to 0..1, so the same few weights decide every connection of the diagram.

    Appetite
    (HP / 50) ^ 0.75 for each animal: a 50 HP animal eats 1 a day, a Brontosaurus 2.2, a Pegomastax 0.5.
    Food
    Plant eaters: chunks × supply × fertility, times dry without surface water. Hunters: carry × appetite of their prey. Flyers and the animals of the sea take fixed shares of the supply.
    Pressure
    p = appetite ÷ food.
    Room
    clamp(1 − p). A fed animal with a mate in the region has a young with daily odds fecundity ÷ span × room, where the land can feed it too; it joins a group of its kind that has room, or founds one.
    Meal
    clamp(2 − p): the odds that the day's meal is found. Found, the record steps towards fed; missed, towards starving, where it dies with daily odds starve.
    Hunted
    Daily odds kill × tempo × Σ clamp(p of its hunters), with tempo = 1 ÷ (0.5 + HP / 100): the more hunters their prey has to carry, the more often it is taken, and giants less often than the small.
    Age
    As coded: the span of a species is 60 × (0.5 + HP / 100) game days, and an animal dies a quarter to five quarters of it after it appeared.
    Arrivals
    A class below its quota of groups is allowed the quota in refill days; at its quota it still meets wanderers, the quota in wander days. Of a group only as many come as keep p ≤ 1. The quota is the game's, times fertility.

    Two bounds hold whatever the weights. No young is born and no animal arrives that would take p above 1, so plant eaters never outgrow what the land grows, and hunters never outgrow what their prey carries. A kind passes its food only when the food itself shrinks (its prey is eaten, the water is gone); then meals are missed and it starves back under.

    Weights

    Move one and the region is lived through again. What is changed here stays in this browser; Save weights writes the file the next build of this page starts from.

      Recipes

      Workstations you read like a map

      The crafting rework (P14): no bench keeps the 3×3 grid. Each one opens a graph of what it makes over half the screen, set in a carved baroque frame with the bench's sigil in its cartouche, and a recipe can ask for more than nine items, so no vanilla table can make it. Six benches run this screen in game (Armoury, Working Station, Saddlery, Mortar & Pestle, Medicine Bench, Smithing Table); the Campfire, the Forge, the Stonecutter and the machine line are designed here and not built yet.

      Every recipe is made where it would really be made, so twelve benches share the %%RECIPES_FAMILIES%% recipe families: the Armoury makes every weapon, tool and armour piece (%%RECIPES_COUNT%% recipes over %%RECIPES_MATERIALS%% materials), the Working Station what is put together by hand (carpentry, textiles, camp gear, accessories, redstone), the Saddlery stitches a saddle for each of the 41 species, unlocked at a level that grows with the animal, the Campfire cooks, the Stonecutter cuts stone, the Mortar & Pestle grinds, the Primitive Forge smelts ore and fires clay and glass, the Medicine Bench makes remedies, the Smithing Table hammers metal goods and trims, and the Iron Age machine line works the steel: the Mechanical Press (which also makes paper and books and packs blocks) rolls sheet and wire, the Milling Machine pipes and engines, the Cutting Machine screws and sawn planks.

      Inventory
      Level and inventory exist only in this preview.

      The graph

      Materials float like notes in Obsidian, dimmed until the player's permanent level unlocks them. Click one and what it makes grows out of it and pushes the rest aside; a double ring is a group that opens the same way. Click a recipe and its ingredients grow beside it, each marked with the amount it needs, red while short. Names show only on hover; drag any node, click empty space or press Escape to step back.

      Crafting

      Click the selected recipe again, or Craft: the result goes straight into the inventory. Shift makes as many as the inventory pays for, and the amount box multiplies the cost. A node with dots under it holds variants (wood types, colours, stone kinds): scroll over it, or use the arrows in the bar, to switch.

      One source for page and mod

      The benches live in Ark/design/workstations: one design file per bench, graph_style.json for the look, frames from tools/build_workstation_crests.py. tools/workstation_plan.py places every recipe, the furnace and stonecutter ones too, and lists any that no bench would show; the page runs the same deterministic graph code as the mod (tools/workstation_graph.js, WorkstationGraph.java), in the same units, font and icons.
