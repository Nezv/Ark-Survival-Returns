One animal, rendered and unrendered

A wild animal is a body while a player is near and a record on the wildlife register otherwise. Rendered, it runs the behavior model; unrendered, the existence model, which settles whether it fed, bred, was hunted or died. The two columns take the same five steps, side by side.

Existence Model

Rules
Arrivals

The first rules: the rounds of SilentLife the game ran before the bounded model (commit %%EXISTENCE_COMMIT%%), kept as silentRules = ODDS with their own constants (listed under Parameters). Under them the game takes new groups in only where a player is near, so Arrivals starts off: this is the land nobody visits. Switch it on for land a player keeps coming back to.

The animal while nobody is near: a record with no body, costing no server time between rounds. Here the rounds run without the game, years in a second, to tune the weights until a region keeps within its bounds.

%%EXISTENCE_BEYOND%%

No record is dropped: an animal a client was sent stays on the register until an end on record (hunted, of age, starved, tamed or removed), and none despawns the vanilla way.

2Population historythe life of a record

Between two rounds a record is fed, hungry or starving. It comes to the region with its group or is born there, and ends hunted, of age or starved. Each connection carries its weight: how often it was taken in the runs.

    4Modelthe bounded functions

    What the game runs beyond the loaded land (spawning.silentRules = BOUNDED, BoundedLife.java). Each round the region reduces its register to one pressure per kind of eater, and every daily odd is a straight line through it, clamped to 0..1.

    Appetite
    (HP / 50) ^ 0.75 for each animal: a 50 HP animal eats 1 a day, a Brontosaurus 2.2, a Pegomastax 0.5.
    Food
    Plant eaters: chunks × supply × fertility, times dry without surface water; in the game the chunks are those of the region that were seen loaded and showed open ground. Hunters: carry × appetite of their prey. Flyers and the animals of the sea take fixed shares of the supply.
    Pressure
    p = appetite ÷ food.
    Room
    clamp(1 − p). A fed animal with a mate in the region has a young with daily odds fecundity ÷ span × room, where the land can feed it too; it joins a group of its kind that has room, or founds one.
    Meal
    clamp(2 − p): the odds that the day's meal is found. Found, the record steps towards fed; missed, towards starving, where it dies with daily odds starve.
    Hunted
    Daily odds kill × tempo × Σ clamp(p of its hunters), with tempo = 1 ÷ (0.5 + HP / 100): the more hunters their prey has to carry, the more often it is taken, and giants less often than the small.
    Age
    As under the first rules: the span of a species is 60 × (0.5 + HP / 100) game days, and an animal dies a quarter to five quarters of it after it appeared.
    Arrivals
    A class below its quota of groups is allowed the quota in refill days; at its quota it still meets wanderers, the quota in wander days. Of a group only as many come as keep p ≤ 1. The quota is the game's, times fertility.

    Two bounds hold whatever the weights. No young is born and no animal arrives that would take p above 1, so plant eaters never outgrow what the land grows, and hunters never outgrow what their prey carries. A kind passes its food only when the food itself shrinks (its prey is eaten, the water is gone); then meals are missed and it starves back under.

    5Parametersthe weights

    Move one and the region is lived through again. Changes stay in this browser; Save weights writes the file the next build of this page starts from. The game reads spawning.boundedLife in the server config, whose defaults these are.

      The workstation screens

      Every recipe is made where it would really be made: %%RECIPES_FAMILIES%% recipe families over the inventory and twelve benches. The inventory and six benches run this screen in game (Armoury, Working Station, Saddlery, Mortar & Pestle, Medicine Bench, Smithing Table); the others are designed here and not built yet.       Inventory
      Level and inventory exist only in this preview.

      The graph

      Materials float like notes in Obsidian, dimmed until the player's level unlocks them. Click one and what it makes grows out of it; a double ring is a group. Click a recipe and its ingredients grow beside it, red while short. Drag any node; click empty space or press Escape to step back.

      Crafting

      Click the selected recipe again, or Craft: the result goes into the inventory. Shift makes as many as the inventory pays for. A node with dots under it holds variants: scroll over it to switch.
