Wildlife

Forty-one creatures, built from ARK skeletons

Every creature keeps its original ARK bone hierarchy and animation clips, rebuilt as Minecraft cuboids and rendered with GeckoLib. Five coloured skin variants each, herds, hunters, nests, sea creatures and cold-climate species. The pips show the first danger rank where each one appears.

%%CREATURES%%
World

A map with hidden danger

The Overworld repeats three danger zones in a symmetric grid: the easy zone around the spawn holds a fifth of the land, the dangerous and the deadly zone two fifths each. Curved borders make the pattern feel random on foot. The zone decides which species spawn and how strong they are. Players only see it through the Xaero map overlay, and that overlay can be locked until the tribe tames a creature from the deadly zone. This map runs the same formula as the mod. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom.

Move over the map

Habitats

Danger and wild levels come only from the zone. The biome decides who lives there: each species has a range of biome kinds that suits it, grazers on open ground, browsers and ambushers under trees, crocodilians on the banks, runners on dry scrub, fishers on the coast, and the snow keeps its own residents. Wildlife is met the way livestock is in the vanilla game, which has no farm animals here: about ten groups within 128 blocks of a player, a herd, a pack or a lone animal every 70 blocks or so, each at its full size, kept apart from one another, hunters about a quarter of the groups by day and by night, and no species twice beside itself. The land ahead of a walking player fills first. The Biomes page shows every biome with its residents.

%%HABITATS%%

Land habitats

Herds keep home ranges near water and predators patrol and hunt. Each 256-block region keeps its own balance of predators and prey, which keeps cycling while nobody is near; hunting thins a region and it recovers over the following days. No chunk is ever forced to load.

Flyers and nests

Each flyer keeps one local nest on cliffs or shores. It perches, forages and returns home, and its eggs can be collected.

Sea and swamp

Sea creatures keep saved home pools. Crocodilians and Titanoboa hunt shorelines, and cold species drink from snow and ice.

Behaviour

Animals that pace themselves

Every creature runs a two-level state machine: an intent (roam, hunt, flee, sleep, drink...) and the animation-timed action that carries it out (notice, roar, chase, look around...). How much of it runs depends on the distance to the nearest player, so a valley of herds costs little until you walk into it, and herd mates react with their own delays instead of moving like one machine.

%%BEHAVIORTIERS%%

Each tier border has a margin of %%BEHAVIORMARGIN%%, so a creature on the line does not flicker between tiers. A hit, an alarm, a target, a rider, a tame or torpor always runs full detail, and hunger and thirst only change there.

The day of a land animal

Each animal shifts its own clock by up to half a minute, so a herd dozes off and wakes one by one.

Each age starts at one gate and splits into its paths (four in the Prehistoric, three after), and every path must be finished to open the next age. Progress is shared by your FTB team. Survival systems

Built for a tribe of two or more

Taming and torpor

Each creature tames one of three ways: passive feeding, knockout with narcoberries and tranquilizer arrows, or aerial feeding by appetite. Favourite foods tame faster, and every creature has a measured rider seat.

Weight and cargo

Carried mass shows on a HUD gauge. Past capacity you slow down, and overloaded flyers refuse to take off. Harnesses unlock each creature's hold, with one-click Load and Unload to nearby chests.

Working tames

Order a Triceratops to forage plants or an Ankylosaurus to mine stone and ore. They work only while a tribe member supervises, and they never touch blocks players placed.

The downed state

A lethal hit starts a bleed-out timer instead of killing you. A tribemate can revive you with a fiber bandage before it runs out.

Keratin and melee

Keratin makes the first armour set and the Keratin Spear. Better Combat gives every Ark weapon its own swing: the spear stabs two-handed with extra reach, knives slash as daggers.

Tribes

FTB Teams parties share riding, cargo, orders and work permissions on each other's tames, plus the Chronicle and the journal.

The First Guardian

Carry an Allosaur Heart to an Ancient Remnants monolith to wake a Guardian Giganotosaurus. It resets if you retreat and can't be farmed, and it drops the Workshop Schematic that opens the workshop age.

Night and behaviour

Creatures forage, drink, rest, sleep, flee and hunt on a day-night cycle. Carnivore eyes glow in the dark, and wild creatures grow stronger with level.

Spatial audio

Built-in reverb and occlusion, footsteps that match the material underfoot, and ambient soundscapes, all in one client-only engine.

Carcasses and cooking

Dinosaurs drop seven meat families plus leather, bone and feathers; horned, plated and beaked ones also drop keratin. Cook meat on the Stone Fire, or hang it on a rack and let it cure to Dried Meat III.

Camp and blocks

Every station is a real object

Original low-poly models built from Minecraft cuboids, with no custom rendering library. Every card is rendered from the model the game ships, with the same camera and light. Stations only work in loaded chunks, so being away never produces a windfall.

%%BLOCKS%%
Recipes

Workstations you read like a map

The crafting rework (P14, in design): no bench keeps the 3×3 grid. Each one opens a graph of what it makes over half the screen, set in a carved baroque frame with the bench's sigil in its cartouche, and a recipe can ask for more than nine items, so no vanilla table can make it. Every recipe is made where it would really be made, so eleven benches share the %%RECIPES_FAMILIES%% recipe families: the Armoury makes every weapon, tool and armour piece (%%RECIPES_COUNT%% recipes over %%RECIPES_MATERIALS%% materials), the Working Station what is put together by hand (carpentry, textiles, camp gear, accessories, redstone), the Campfire cooks, the Stonecutter cuts stone, the Mortar & Pestle grinds, the Primitive Forge smelts ore and fires clay and glass, the Medicine Bench makes remedies, the Smithing Table hammers metal goods and trims, and the Iron Age machine line works the steel: the Mechanical Press (which also makes paper and books and packs blocks) rolls sheet and wire, the Milling Machine pipes and engines, the Cutting Machine screws and sawn planks. The game keeps its current recipes until the whole rework is designed.

Inventory
Level and inventory exist only in this preview.

The graph

Materials float like notes in Obsidian, dimmed until the player's permanent level unlocks them. Click one and what it makes grows out of it and pushes the rest aside; a double ring is a group that opens the same way. Click a recipe and its ingredients grow beside it, each marked with the amount it needs, red while short. Names show only on hover; drag any node, click empty space or press Escape to step back.

Crafting

Click the selected recipe again, or Craft: the result goes straight into the inventory. Shift makes as many as the inventory pays for, and the amount box multiplies the cost. A node with dots under it holds variants (wood types, colours, stone kinds): scroll over it, or use the arrows in the bar, to switch.

One source for page and mod

The benches live in Ark/design/workstations: one design file per bench, graph_style.json for the look, frames from tools/build_workstation_crests.py. tools/workstation_plan.py places every recipe, the furnace and stonecutter ones too, and lists any that no bench would show; the page runs the same deterministic graph code the mod will port to Java (tools/workstation_graph.js), in the same units, font and icons.

Accessories

Gear worn on the body

Every Curios slot is in use: two head slots, necklace, back, body, two bracelets, hands, two rings, belt, legs, two feet, a charm and the universal curio slot. Each accessory is a small model hung on the player's own bones, so it walks, sneaks, swims and rides with the wearer, spreads over armour, and shows on the first-person arms. Renders below come from the shipped models.

Primitive gadgets

What prehistoric people actually wore, made from creature parts and camp materials: slit goggles, Ötzi's pack frame, snowshoes, bone skates, a Beaker-culture wristguard, the Ishango tally bone. The stalker set (hood, ghillie wrap, moccasins) hides each sense the creatures use: scent, sight and hearing.

Models

Every authored 3D model, live

Built from Minecraft cuboids and drawn here from the files the mod ships: the same elements, UVs and textures, in three.js. Machines run their real animation rules, armour turns on a walking player, weapons turn in place. Drag any model to turn it. Offline, each card shows a still render instead.

Machines

The Iron Age line (planned): press, mill and cutter share one 1×2 chassis with a belt that hands items from machine to machine. Heat at the back sets the speed, like Create's tiers: cold stops the line, superheated doubles it. Pick the heat under each machine.

Interface rework

Carved stone, moss and ember

The Ark UI resource pack re-skins the whole vanilla interface to match this page, so shaders and the menus share one look. Containers become light limestone, so vanilla's dark titles stay readable. Integrations

Every mod in the pack, and what Ark does with it

Each integration is one row in the management dashboard. Official releases are pinned by SHA-512 and rebranded "Ark - Name" with the Ark icon and a credit line; the original license and mod id stay intact. Where Ark needs code changes, the mod is built from its own source with Ark patches (Ark/pack/patches). python Ark/tools/build_pack.py assembles everything into a Modrinth pack.
